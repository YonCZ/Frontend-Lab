One note, six neighbours

You write the note. Cairn reads the names, dates and subjects inside it and points it at the notes that share them. Every link is a suggestion you can keep or cut.

A note is rarely about one thing. The meeting mentions a person, the person has three other notes, and the deposit turns up again a month later in a message from the landlord.

Cairn keeps those threads without asking you to maintain them. There is no folder to file a note into, and no tag taxonomy to remember.

  • Suggestions appear while you type, not in a cleanup session later
  • Each link keeps the sentence it came from, so you can check it
  • Unlinked mentions surface before you think to search

Answers that stay on your Mac

Cairn runs its search and its answers on the machine in front of you. Notes are indexed locally, and nothing is uploaded to answer a question.

Everything Cairn knows is in the folder you chose. The index is a file beside it. Close the lid and the library keeps working on a plane, a train, or a bad hotel connection.

The first index of 10,000 notes takes about four minutes. After that, new notes are indexed as you write them.
